**19 earlier-review misses, 11 introduced during repair, zero unresolved for the reported reproduction paths.** These are 30 distinct defects across 29 numbered Must Fixes. INTEGRATION-ROLLUPS M2 accounts for 15 of the misses. SHARED-UI M2 has the reverse pattern, with seven of its nine defects introduced during repairs. SHARED-UI M3 has two misses and three introduced defects. Both review coverage and repair regressions contributed to the repeated failures. In particular, a review can cover every acceptance item and still miss the combination of route, state and failure that exposes a defect.

## What the historical builds showed

I used the reviewed commit on each claim in the module's `ACCEPTED.json`. I checked out the earliest earlier claim and replayed the later finding against it. When the initial control passed or lacked the specific mechanism, I checked the intervening claims and source changes for the first failing revision. The commit column is the first confirmed occurrence within that window. An issue present at the first review may predate that commit. "Introduced" means it arrived after the first review, even if an intervening reviewer subsequently missed it. A different failure on the same route does not count as reproducing the later finding.

I ran historical production builds in detached throwaway worktrees and used Chrome for canvas interaction, mounted refresh, history and Search. I used scripts against the historical reader and validator for inventory issues. Browser checks used scratch copies of fixtures. For recovery checks, I generated real stale-revision responses and held or substituted the follow-up response where the reviewer had injected a fault. I split INTEGRATION-ROLLUPS M2 round 2's fifth Must Fix into its message-evidence and cache-purge defects because they have different triggers. SHARED-UI M1 failed its first review at `5d73e891` and passed its second at `cf243dcc`. It has no finding in the requested window.

## What the pattern suggests

The most consistent gap in the review records is at the level of scenarios. A checklist item like "links are selectable" was exercised without proving that each link had a distinct hit target when several links used the same geometry. A checklist item about revision recovery was exercised through one entry point or transport without always checking Search, hover, a failed replacement, or a mounted view with existing state. The records do not support a general conclusion that reviewers stopped at the first Must Fix or reviewed only the patch. Several continued after reporting a blocker and most gave a judgement against every acceptance item. That breadth still left correlated cases untested.

The repair histories point to a separate change for Orchestrate. When a fix touches a shared mechanism, the next review should preserve the previous failing reproduction as a passing control and test neighbouring cases with the same mechanism. For link routing that means direction, depth and overlapping hit targets together. For revision changes it means each recovery entry point, success and failure, and what remains visible or actionable before the replacement settles. Recording those cases against the exact claim would distinguish an omitted situation, an attempted check with incomplete evidence, and an observed regression in a repair.
