# Thrum delivery, 16 Nov 16:00 to 18 Nov 05:00 UTC: what to change in the skills

Source: the Delivery Lead session in ~/Workspace/thrum (transcript e5ff9d25, about 1,970 turns, four context compactions), read in four slices, with git on origin/main checked for what landed overnight.

## What the 36 hours produced

- **Overnight (21:00 to 05:00):** five milestones passed (CREATOR M3, REVIEW-ENGINE M4 and M5, SCENE-VIEWS M1 and M2). About seven reviews failed, two milestones blocked, about 16 design reviews asked for changes.
- **Builders running:** about six at 21:43, two from 03:21. Codex weekly use went from 64% to 69%, although the human asked for "the most number of workers online".
- **Pace:** most milestones fail their first review and pass the second. The lead put about 60% of jobs as failed-and-rerun. V1 is "more like the end of the month than this weekend".

## Findings, ranked by cost

### 1. The quality bar was set too high, and nothing in the skills asked whether it was worth its cost

The human wanted "top quality atmosphere" and "the modern technique". Planners turned that into f64 physical-reference gates and exact image baselines. The cost showed up as repeated fix rounds, and CLOUDS M3 took six visual attempts before anyone compared against a reference and found a design error (5.5% light where 84% was right). The lead defended the bar to the human at 21:49 ("the whole pitch: an engine that's correct and fast") and agreed to lower it three minutes later, when the human asked.

- **plan-module:** "What a proof costs" weighs time and locks, not strictness. Add a step where the planner sets the bar by how the result will be judged: for anything judged by eye, done-when items say "looks as good as a named shipped example, within a timing limit", and a reference is a tool for finding causes, not a gate. Keep exact proofs for things that break silently (networking, determinism, formats, contracts).
- **interview-me:** it already asks for "a shipped example of the quality wanted". Carry that example into the brief as the bar, so planners don't upgrade "top quality" to "physically exact".
- **build (the lead):** when fail-and-rerun runs high for a day, the lead should raise the cost of the standard with the human, not wait to be asked.

### 2. A project-wide change of standard used up the night

The bar change went to one planner at 21:57. It edited six modules, all six lost their design approval, and all six re-reviews asked for changes (one because the planner had applied the bar only partly). Planners run one at a time, so that queue filled the night. Meanwhile the brief exempted milestones already being built, so none of the overnight builders' work changed, including the APPLE-PRESENTATION M3 item that asked a human to watch clips.

- **plan-module / build:** say how to roll out a change of standard: apply it to each module's next milestone as it starts, skip a full design re-review for edits that only loosen a standard, and don't exempt a milestone whose blocking item the change makes pointless.

### 3. Planners queue behind one repository-wide lock, in no particular order, and the lead can't see the queue

The CLOUDS must-fix planner, on the V1 path, queued at 21:54 and was still waiting at 04:31, while MESH-SHADING and RAY-TRACING (both after V1) took the lock for two or three rounds each. The lead found the holder by reading process trees and lock files.

- **agent_loop.py:** order the planner queue by critical path (or let the lead set a priority), and have `status` name the lock holder, the waiters and how long each has waited.

### 4. Settings are read from a checkout that can be far behind main

The model change to gpt-6.2 was committed in a side worktree. `run` read ORCHESTRATE.md from the main checkout, 1,466 commits behind, so builders relaunched on gpt-6.1-sol; reviews read each module branch's own stale copy and ran on gpt-6-astra. The lead told the human "Each moves to 6.2 at its next launch" without checking, then ran `pkill -f "codex exec.*"` and killed five builders mid-run. `main-check` from the same stale checkout returned no checks at all.

- **agent_loop.py:** read settings from origin/main (or refuse with a clear message when the checkout is behind), and do the same for reviews on module branches.
- **build:** after any settings change, confirm the model of the next launch before saying it has changed; stop specific runs with `pause`, never by process pattern.

### 5. The lead rebuilt the missing parts of the script by hand

The scratchpad holds about 30 helper scripts. The ones that matter, and what each says is missing:

| Helper | What it does | Missing in agent_loop.py |
|---|---|---|
| `runres.py`, `runnext.sh` | Runs a result's `next.argv`, rewriting `/2.3.6/` to `/2.3.7/`, adding `env` because argv starts with `ORCHESTRATE_VISIBLE=1`, pinning models, adding `--more-builders` | A `resume RESULT` command whose argv runs as given, uses the current script version and keeps the run's host, model and standing approvals |
| `r6.sh`, `runfable.sh`, `runclaude.sh` and others | Host, model, effort and quota wording per launch | Per-role defaults and a quota policy in ORCHESTRATE.md, plus a standing "more builders" approval with a quota ceiling |
| `maincheck6.sh`, disk loop | `main-check --due` every 30 min; free-space watch | A watch mode for main checks and disk, with clean-up of `.git/orchestrate/verify` (90 GB) and finished-milestone `tmp/` |
| caffeinate timers | Wake the display for visible runs | Wake the display itself on `ORCHESTRATE_VISIBLE` runs; the AGENTS.md instruction was not followed by reviewers |

### 6. Main broke three times on files the agents themselves wrote, and recovery needed hand work

A reviewer's report linked seven uncommitted captures; a reviewer pushed eight captures over 2 MiB; plan links were copied verbatim into STATUS.md, breaking `check-docs`. Each blocked every merge. Recovery then hit two script defects: a module blocked mid-merge keeps plan files on its branch, so both `run` and `adopt` refuse; and a module stays Blocked after its planner or a hand merge fixes it, needing `unblock`.

- **agent_loop.py:** check a review or plan commit's own links and evidence sizes before it lands; rewrite or drop plan links when copying into STATUS.md; lift Blocked when its cause is fixed.

### 7. Stalls were waited out and then misdiagnosed

CREATOR M3 stalled twice for four hours each. The lead blamed the sleeping display and told the builder so as fact ("stalled for 4 hours because the display went to sleep"). The real cause was a Codex thread cut off by a session restart, with the app holding its messages. The script already knows hung threads but waited out the two-hour turn limit and reported "no progress".

- **agent_loop.py:** notice a rollout file that has stopped changing, name the likely cause and the recovery (`unqueue`, `--fresh`).
- **prompting-agents:** in a steer, state a cause only after checking it; otherwise say it is a guess. This is the existing "verified apart from suspected" guidance applied to what the lead sends, not only to what it asks for.

### 8. Done-when items that the named prover could not prove

- CREATOR M3.1 needed a reviewer who had not read the design, but the review skill loads the engineering docs first. Four reviews failed on that alone; no product defect was found. The lead solved it with its own clean-worktree subagent.
- EFFECT-GRAPH M4 blocked twice on timing items that contradicted each other; PUDDLES M2.11 failed three times before the builder called it under-specified; CULLING reviewers judged frame pacing on items about geometry.

- **plan-module:** check each item can be proved by the role named to prove it, and that the items in a milestone agree with each other.
- **review-milestone:** support a fresh-reader item, run first in a separate session; when the reviewer can't run a proof, say so after the first failure; judge only what an item states.

### 9. Restarts and compactions lost state

The Claude Code update killed all 12 background tasks. The lead relaunched without first reading results already on main, which caused a duplicate design review that held the module lock and an INVALID re-review. One compaction summary listed an answered question as open, and the lead answered it again.

- **build:** after a restart, read results and main before relaunching anything. Keep open questions and their answers in the lead's notes file, which survived compactions well.

### 10. The overnight check-in only checked health

The hourly prompt asked the lead to start builders for ready modules, check for stalls, disk and quota, and kill only specific PIDs. Seven ticks reported "clean" while two builders ran and quota went unused, a question to the human held back six modules, and the V1 planner sat in the queue. It never asked what was limiting throughput.

- **prompting-agents:** a check-in for an unattended run should name the outcome it is protecting (here: V1 critical path moving, Codex quota used) and ask what is holding that back, not only whether anything is broken. The goal-writing guidance covers this for long runs; it should cover recurring check-ins too.
- **build:** when the lead knows a human answer will block several modules overnight, put that question to the human before they go to bed.

## What worked and should become the default

- **Briefs that try to kill the idea first.** The atmosphere planner was told to check whether the colour change was visible and drop it if not. The theses planner was told the human wanted to know "if they change our thinking, not a summary".
- **Steers with a decision rule.** "If M2.11 is physically impossible as written, end BLOCKED and say so for the planner instead of working around it." "If production matches, that is a planner question: end Blocked with the numbers." The MESH-SHADING and RAY-TRACING planners' findings dropped 7→6→2 and 5→4→2 after steers of this kind.
- **Cheap readers.** Sonnet at medium, fixed output format, "Don't invent", reply only "done". The lead's context stayed light.
- **Quota-aware model choice,** stated each time ("Opus 5.5 at high, because Fable is at 89%"), with an 88% stop for Claude work.
- **Candour with the human.** "No. At today's pace, V1 is more like the end of the month." "Mostly state of the art, adopted well, not beyond it." "I was wrong about why CREATOR M3 stalled." It owned the pkill mistake.

Weak points in how it talked to the human: a status message on nearly every notification although the standing rule says digests are async; a few script terms reaching the human ("Exit 75"); one question asked three times over Remote Control; and the walkthrough's "Cutting edge:" field, which invited every module to claim it was.
