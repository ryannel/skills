# Character-LoRA dataset curation on modern DiTs — research harvest, 2026-09-09

Scope: Krea 2 first, then FLUX.2, Qwen-Image, Z-Image, and by analogy Wan 2.2 and MiniMax H3. Research notes only — no skill prose.

**Method and its limits.** Reddit was hard-blocked on every route tried, so the r/StableDiffusion first-hand layer is absent or reaches us as search snippets, flagged as such; the web-search budget also ran out partway. What survived is the verifiable layer: trainer docs (musubi-tuner, ai-toolkit, diffusion-pipe), vendor docs (Krea, BFL, fal), HF cards and discussions, login-free Civitai articles, GitHub, arXiv. **No source found anywhere ran a controlled dataset ablation holding everything else equal.** Every N-vs-M claim below is a loss-curve comparison, a retrain with more than one variable moved, or an assertion. That is the ceiling here.

## 1. Dataset size

**Modal answer: 20–30 images, unmoved.** ~30 for character, 50 for style, 100 for concepts `[community-consensus — Boerboer, Civitai, 2026-06-22]` https://civitai.com/articles/31678/a-complete-beginners-guide-to-local-anima-lora-training; "20 high-quality is enough" for Krea 2 `[community — chengyansen-ai/krea2-lora-training v0.4.0, read 2026-09-09]` https://github.com/chengyansen-ai/krea2-lora-training; 20–30 for a person on Krea 2 `[single-report — promptdexter, 2026-07-24]` https://promptdexter.com/blog/krea2-lora-training-guide.

**Official floors are far below practice.** Krea's hosted trainer requires "at least three" `[official — krea.ai, 2026-05-21]` https://www.krea.ai/blog/krea-2-lora-training. fal's FLUX.2 trainer says "20 to 1,000" `[official, 2025-11-25]` https://blog.fal.ai/training-flux-2-loras — too wide to carry signal. BFL is tighter: **15–40** `[official — Batifol, BFL, 2026-06-04]` https://huggingface.co/blog/black-forest-labs/flux-2-klein-lora.

**Modern DiTs need fewer images than SDXL-era; Z-Image has the lowest floor.** Nine 1024² images "were sufficient to imprint a subject reliably" on Z-Image Turbo `[single-report — Shawn, HF blog, 2025-12-02]` https://huggingface.co/blog/content-and-code/training-a-lora-for-z-image-turbo. A per-family floor table gives **Z-Image 12, Krea 2 / FLUX.1 / FLUX.2 Klein 15, SDXL 20** — SDXL the only family with a higher floor `[community tooling — perfectgf/lora-dataset-studio]` https://github.com/perfectgf/lora-dataset-studio/blob/main/docs/DATASET_GUIDE.md.

**The knee is disputed across an order of magnitude, all single-report.** ~30 ("going above 30 rarely helps") `[apatero, 2026-03-04]` https://apatero.com/blog/flux-2-pro-lora-training-character-consistency-2026; ~50 *unless the character has many distinct visual states* `[apatero, 2026-01-06]` https://apatero.com/blog/qwen-image-2512-character-lora-training-real-people-guide-2025 — the most useful framing found; ~100, where 25 images gives a "recognizable character but inconsistent details… weak outside" weights 0.8–0.9 `[apatero, 2025-10-16]` https://apatero.com/blog/lora-training-complete-guide-headshots-body-shots-2025 (predates FLUX.2/Krea 2; likely SDXL behaviour); several hundred — 474 character + 348 regularisation `[JahJedi, 2026-06-26]` https://huggingface.co/JahJedi/krea2-character-lora-recipe.

**The one published same-recipe comparison does not show what it appears to.** JahJedi Run A (~800 pairs, 13,152 steps) final loss 0.0227; Run B (127 pairs, 2,032 steps) 0.0522. That is **training loss, not identity fidelity**, on a family where loss does not track visual quality (§4). The bigger set trained longer; nothing more follows.

**First-hand Krea 2 datapoints cluster at 20–36.** 36 images at 1024², 3,000 steps, 12 GB `[natural-light, 2026-07-01]` https://huggingface.co/krea/Krea-2-Turbo/discussions/10; 20 images, 4,000 steps but "2000 already sufficient" `[かみもと, 2026-06-28]` https://note.com/sepiablue/n/nbc355cc7e114. **Small-and-clean beats large-and-noisy, with no dissent found** — Krea: "a cleaner set that repeats the same subject usually teaches the model more clearly than a large, mixed dataset" `[official]`.

## 2. Content and coverage

**The best-argued identity ratio is 0.33, not 40/40/20.** ~33% identity-emphasis (close-ups, head-and-shoulders, clean backgrounds) / ~67% in-context (full-body, varied environments, poses, outfits), "anywhere from 0.25 to 0.4 works," over 30–60 images `[community-consensus — neonkisu, Civitai, 2026-06-15]` https://civitai.com/articles/31467/dataset-prep-for-character-loras-how-the-033-identity-ratio-actually-works. Its value is naming both failure modes: **95% close-up** gives "great faces but loses the character's body type, outfit consistency, and pose flexibility"; **95% in-context** gives a face that "drifts toward generic anime when the prompt zooms in." Competing figures: 50/50 headshots-to-body `[apatero, 2025-10-16]`; "at least 30% profile shots" `[curated, not first-hand — sozee, 2025-12-18/2026-09-02]` https://sozee.ai/resources/lora-photorealism-training-dataset-quality/. **No source proposes 40/40/20** — the literature is two-bucket, with tooling tracking four (face / bust / body / back) but setting no targets.

**The ratio is really a function of face pixels.** Sharpest evidence is a negative result on MiniMax H3: 32 images mixing full-body, upper-body and close-ups at **512×512** gave almost no likeness; a retrain on **31 face-focused images** on white backgrounds was a "substantial improvement," because at 512² full-body framing "the face area ends up being small" `[single-report — かみもと, 2026-08-07]` https://note.com/sepiablue/n/nf0763f854580. The same author later fixed it another way on musubi: **1,000 steps on full-body, then +500 on a face-only set** `[2026-08-17]` https://note.com/sepiablue/n/nc1ce412d6ea2. Read together: the lower the training resolution, the harder the set must skew to close-ups. That resolves the apparent conflict rather than contradicting 0.33.

**Counter-datapoint on the image side.** A successful Krea 2 Turbo run used "17 full-body images, 17 bust shots, and 2 face close-up shots" — ~6% close-up — with good body consistency and face consistency only "acceptable" `[natural-light, 2026-07-01]`. At 1024² bust shots appear to substitute for close-ups, and the reported weakness is where you would predict.

**Constants-vs-variables has unanimous agreement.** A LoRA learns whatever is "constant across your images and NOT described in the captions. Keep the subject constant, vary everything else, and never describe the subject" `[lora-dataset-studio]`. Krea from the other side: "if an image has a detail you don't want the LoRA to learn, call it out in the caption" `[official]`. Canonical example: caption "black jacket" *only* where the jacket appears, so the model has evidence it is separable.

**Exclusions, high agreement:** group shots unless croppable to one subject; faces occluded by sunglasses, masks, heavy hair or extreme filters; beauty filters; motion blur; heavy JPEG; low resolution; watermarks and readable text. Two specific to real people: wildly divergent lighting ("confuse the model about skin tone") and wide age spans — "if your training data spans 20 years, the LoRA will average features" `[apatero, 2026-01-06]`. Governing heuristic: **"when in doubt, it's usually better to leave an image out than hope the trainer ignores it."**

**Duplicates and multi-crops.** "25 varied images beat 60 near-duplicates every time," with a shipped pre-flight check flagging pairs `[lora-dataset-studio]`; "ten images that are slightly cropped versions of the same source will produce a LoRA that only generates that one pose." The carve-out is editing that removes noise — cropping empty borders, splitting a group photo. **Mixed aspect buckets cost ~15–17% wall time** (1.40–1.46 vs 1.20 s/it) from cuDNN kernel re-selection, but "variety in poses and crops is more valuable than 17% off the clock" `[JahJedi, 2026-06-26]`.

## 3. Resolution — the rabbit hole

Most of the answer here is mechanical rather than empirical.

**Trainers downscale; they never upscale.** ai-toolkit: "Images are never upscaled but they are downscaled and placed in buckets for batching. You do not need to crop/resize your images" `[official — ostris/ai-toolkit README, read 2026-09-09]` https://github.com/ostris/ai-toolkit. musubi's `bucket_no_upscale` "allows upscaling images that are smaller than the bucket target. **If all your images are high-resolution, they'll only ever be downscaled, so this setting has no practical effect on those datasets**" `[community — AInVFX, 2026-04-06]` https://github.com/AInVFX/AInVFX-News/blob/main/episodes/20260406/. **Consequence: a 4K source and a 1024 source land in the same latent. Upscaled sources add the upscaler's resampling prior and no information.**

**That prior is the mechanism behind "scale skin."** "Upscaling can make dimensions pass a loader check, but it cannot recover evidence that was never captured… Enlarged blur is still blur. **Synthetic detail can become an unwanted texture prior**" `[curated, undated — LocalForge AI]` https://offlinecreator.com/guide/best-image-size-for-lora-training. Restated as "do not upscale low-res images to meet the requirement — you are training on fake detail" `[zsky, 2026-01-26/2026-07-14]` https://zsky.ai/blog/lora-training-guide and "don't upsize: it only memorizes noise" `[WaveSpeedAI, 2026-01-23]` https://wavespeed.ai/blog/posts/blog-wan-2-2-lora-training-settings/.

**A second cause of reticulated skin has nothing to do with upscaling, and matches the reported symptom best.** A trainer chasing skin artifacts blamed freckles, built a freckle-free dataset, and **still got artifacts** — "stretch marks scattered all over the body," appearing "as soon as the character's likeness became reasonably stable." The conclusion was **aliasing** — "similar to the moiré pattern you can see on TV when someone is wearing a checkered shirt" — because the base already renders skin micro-detail well, so training re-learns it and **overtrains the skin** `[single-report — Civitai article 16340; reached via search snippets only, article login-gated behind civitai.red]` https://civitai.com/articles/16340. Two consequences: it is a training-intensity and spatial-frequency problem, not a content one, so removing the trait does not fix it; and it arrives at the same time as good likeness, which is exactly why a successful run reads as "high res made it worse."

**A third cause is over-smoothed sources.** "Training images with over-smoothed skin from beauty retouching or low-resolution sources. The model learns to reproduce the smoothed distribution," fixed with unretouched RAW exports `[sozee]`. Face-restoration passes belong here: they win the file-quality battle and lose the likeness one.

**On Krea 2, one texture complaint is a false positive.** "Raw previews look plastic" is listed as **expected** — "nothing — expected; verify on Turbo, don't overtrain" `[chengyansen-ai v0.4.0]`. Raw is undistilled and flat by design.

**Where the bucket ceiling is.** musubi's Krea 2 schedule is resolution-aware over **256–1280 px**: shift ≈1.6 at 256², ≈2.5 at 1024², ≈3.2 at 1280², with `--timestep_sampling krea2_shift` reproducing it per sample so bucketed datasets shift exactly as K2 shifts at inference. The docs add: "The optimal settings are not yet established; feedback is welcome" `[official — kohya-ss/musubi-tuner docs/krea2.md, read 2026-09-09]` https://github.com/kohya-ss/musubi-tuner/blob/main/docs/krea2.md. So buckets above 1024 are *schedulable* to 1280 — but **no published run shows a win from going there**, and the mechanics above say the extra pixels must be real to buy anything.

**Multi-resolution training is mainstream everywhere except this repo's Krea 2 guidance.** ai-toolkit ships `resolution: [ 512, 768, 1024 ]` for both Flux and Qwen-Image, commented "flux enjoys multiple resolutions" / "qwen image enjoys multiple resolutions" `[official — ai-toolkit config/examples, read 2026-09-09]`. BFL: "start with 512px for faster iterations… use 1024px or higher for final training" `[official — docs.bfl.ml/flux_2/flux2_klein_training]`. Two Krea 2 sources use **512 + 768 + 1024** for "composition, quality, face details respectively" `[chengyansen-ai v0.4.0]`; a dataset tool ships 768+1024 cross-family, dropping to **768-only as a memory lever** ("Krea 2 especially — 1024 saturates a 24 GB card"), not a quality choice `[lora-dataset-studio]`.

**Practical source target: 1024 long edge, native, uncompressed, unretouched.** BFL: "at least 1024 pixels on the long edge" `[official]`. LoKr is reported *more* sensitive to source resolution and JPEG compression than plain LoRA `[chengyansen-ai]`. Nothing above 1024 native has a published win on any of these models.

## 4. Steps, schedule, rank, LR, and the distilled variant

**Train undistilled, infer distilled — every vendor shipping a pair now says so.** Krea: "TRAIN on Raw and RUN on Turbo" `[official]`, restated by kohya `[official — docs/krea2.md]`. BFL: base models "are undistilled and provide higher output diversity, making them ideal starting points for fine-tuning," and at inference the LoRA goes on the distilled model, which is "faster and, in our testing, **usually gives even better results**" `[official, 2026-06-04]`. musubi says the same for Z-Image since Base shipped `[official — docs/zimage.md]`.

**The de-distill adapter is a bounded hack, per its author.** Ostris built it from ~1,000 images generated by the target model, trained at lr 1e-5 until "the samples begin to degrade as distillation breaks down. This is the goal." It "slows this down significantly" rather than fixing it — short runs only `[official — ostris HF card + discussion, ~2026-08-11]` https://huggingface.co/ostris/krea2_turbo_training_adapter. **No first-hand A/B of Raw-trained vs Turbo+adapter-trained on the same dataset exists** — the largest single gap found.

**Steps-per-image is dead.** Same model, same trainer: a 337-image Krea 2 style run used 3,960 steps (~12/image); a 20-image character run used 2,000–4,000 (~100–200/image). **Official budgets are now dataset-size-independent** — BFL quotes 1,500–2,500 for style and **1,500–3,000 for character across a 15–40 image range** `[official — docs.bfl.ml]`; ai-toolkit ships `steps: 2000  # 500 - 4000 is a good range` with no reference to set size `[official]`. Krea 2 in the wild: 2,500 first run or 3,000–4,000 at lr 5e-5 `[chengyansen-ai]`; 1,600–2,000 for a person `[promptdexter]`; "90% likeness is reached by 1750 steps" `[jessikat29, ~2026-08]`.

**Loss is not the selector, and vendors now say so.** "**Watch the samples, not the loss.** Loss keeps dropping well past the point where the images start to overfit… most style LoRAs visually peak around steps 750–1500, not the final checkpoint" `[official — BFL]`. Confirmed on Krea 2: `avr_loss` moved only 0.0774 → 0.0735 across 3,960 steps `[Zaytron40k]` https://huggingface.co/Zaytron40k/krea2-style-v1.

**The cheapest overtrain test is a no-trigger control prompt.** Three prompts per checkpoint at fixed seed with `walk_seed: false`: in-distribution; trigger in a novel scene; and **the same text minus the trigger, which "must stay close to base; if polluted, you overtrained"** `[chengyansen-ai v0.4.0]`. Z-Image adds a second: "if changing the seed gives you the exact same composition, that's also overtraining" `[AInVFX]`.

**Rank.** Krea 2's default is **32/32 across all 264 Linear layers**; the authors' *long-run* config raises rank but narrows scope to the 140 attention projections "to preserve prompt adherence" `[official — docs/krea2.md]` — the cleanest published answer to whether high rank hurts. ai-toolkit ships **rank 16** for Qwen-Image; Z-Image's default is dim 32 / alpha 16. Contrarian: across ~64 dimension-only FLUX.2 runs, **128/64/64/32** beat everything from 32 to 256, called "universally strong" — but that is a *style* LoRA, and BFL's klein config also uses 128/64/64/32, so it corroborates for style only `[single-report — Herbst, 2026-02-06]`.

**Two trainer traps.** On Krea 2, porting FLUX's `sigmoid`/`weighted` timestep type is named the #1 cause of failed runs; the pairing is `timestep_type: linear` with `noise_scheduler: flowmatch` `[chengyansen-ai]`. On Z-Image, **`adamw8bit` reportedly fails on BF16** because small gradient updates are zeroed in low-precision arithmetic — one user got good results at **900 steps** with `optimi.AdamW` (Kahan summation) versus 3,000+ with adamw8bit *and still needing 2.15× LoRA strength at inference*, read as a symptom of the precision bug rather than of Base→Turbo transfer `[community — AInVFX, 2026-04-06]`.

**Strength is a diagnostic.** A finished FLUX.2 style LoRA peaked at **0.73**, usable 0.4–0.75, with "0.8 to 1.0 maximizes texture but starts pulling the image apart" — a LoRA needing 1.0 is a red flag `[Herbst]`.

## 5. Captions

**Caption-the-residual is near-unanimous.** "Whatever you don't caption in your training images gets baked into the trigger word"; omission is "essentially deliberate concept bleed" `[AInVFX, 2026-04-06]`. Krea: "Captions tell the trainer what to learn and what to ignore, so a one-off background or prop doesn't accidentally become part of the LoRA" `[official]`.

**The trigger-token question is open, and the camps split by vendor rather than encoder class.** Against rare tokens: on Krea 2, "random strings are either ignored entirely or learned incredibly poorly, resulting in a trigger word showing as text or watermark" `[promptdexter, 2026-07-24]`. For them, *officially*: BFL tells FLUX.2 klein trainers to "use a made-up trigger word (e.g. `SPR1TE8`, `RISO_PR1NT`) consistently across all captions" `[official, 2026-06-04]` — on a model with an LLM text encoder. Also for: `sks_personname` / `ohwx personname` on Qwen-Image `[apatero, 2026-01-06]`; a non-word token on Z-Image Turbo "to avoid collisions with vocabulary semantics" `[HF blog, 2025-12-02]`. Z-Image adds placement: trigger "at the very start of the caption in natural sentence position, not comma-separated," and **not in double quotes** `[AInVFX]`. **No published A/B of `ohwx` vs a natural name on an LLM-class encoder.**

**Prose vs tags is model-split; mixing is harmless.** Krea 2 and Klein "want natural language, not a comma-separated pile of booru tags" `[promptdexter]`. Z-Image was trained on five caption styles — short tags, short and long natural language, multilingual — so it is tolerant by construction `[AInVFX]`. Official split: Flux 2 "handles longer, more descriptive captions comfortably," while for Qwen Image "concise, precise captions tend to outperform sprawling ones" `[official — Scenario]` https://help.scenario.com/articles/5782148871-advanced-captioning.

**On length, the evidence now runs against evenness.** One source caps at 30–50 words, "absolutely do not exceed 70" `[promptdexter]`. Against: "Caption **variety** matters more than length for Z-Image… a mix of short (5–10 word) and longer (30–50 word) captions for different images improves LoRA generalization significantly. **Don't write identically structured captions for every image**" `[AInVFX]`; Scenario independently lists "same caption on every image" as a failure mode.

**Caption dropout is contested by an order of magnitude.** ai-toolkit ships `caption_dropout_rate: 0.05` `[official]`; the 0.33-ratio article calls **0.3** "the strongest single fix for generalization" `[neonkisu]`. musubi is only now adding it: PR #1084, **draft, opened 2026-08-31, unmerged**, default 0.0, example 0.1, rationale "without it, the model never sees an empty/unconditional caption during training, so guidance can behave poorly at inference." **Hard constraint: rejected for image-conditioned text-encoder caching** (Qwen-Image `--is_edit`, HiDream-O1 control), since the empty-caption embedding would have to vary per image `[official]` https://github.com/kohya-ss/musubi-tuner/pull/1084.

**Caption contextually by shot size** — describe only what is in frame, so close-ups omit full outfit details; an anti-composition-overfit measure `[JahJedi, 2026-06-26]`.

**No external source supports "naming a trait *and* showing it everywhere over-bakes it."** The documented mechanism runs the other way, and the nearest real evidence points opposite: pulling hair colour *out* of a trigger into its own tag caused colour drift, stabilising only when reabsorbed `[single-report — lilting.ch, 53-image experiment, 2026-04-26]` https://lilting.ch/en/articles/wai-anima-lora-caption-rework.

## 6. Synthetic data

**Practitioner ratio numbers are dry** — no dated first-hand report of a character LoRA trained with a measured synthetic:real split and results shown. What exists is the collapse literature, and it splits hard.

**Pessimistic, and directly on the recursive-LoRA case:** in a self-consuming chain of diffusion finetuning, **5% synthetic images suffice to initiate degradation** (CelebA-1k; 10% for Pokemon), and mixing 50% real gives "minimal protection." Visible deterioration by the third iteration; **recall (diversity) collapses before FID moves** `[academic — arXiv 2407.17493]` https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17493v3. *The published skill cites arXiv 2311.12202 for ReDiFine; 2407.17493 is the ID matching this content — verify which is correct.*

**Optimistic, with conditions:** a 2026 survey gives task-dependent safe fractions — creative image generation **60–90% synthetic acceptable with entropy/diversity controls** — under one rule: "always accumulate real data, never replace it… Keep a small set of real human data and never delete it. Use it in every round." The diffusion failure is variance collapse: on synthetic alone "the learned covariance matrix tends toward zero" `[academic — arXiv 2608.21366]` https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21366.

**These reconcile.** 2407.17493 measures a recursive loop with no diversity control — exactly "train v2 on v1's renders." The survey assumes a preserved real anchor and active entropy monitoring. The 5% figure applies to the naive loop; 60–90% only to a gated one.

**The most useful curation mechanism is the generalization-to-memorization transition.** Recursive training moves models from generating novel images to replicating training images; entropy of generated data falls sharply, with **Pearson r = 0.91 between training-set entropy and generalization**, and entropy-based selection slows it `[academic — arXiv 2509.16499, 2025-09-20]` https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16499v1. Foundational: Shumailov et al., tails disappear, defects "irreversible" `[arXiv 2305.17493]`. **This creates a real tension with face-embedding triage** — a similarity gate *reduces* set entropy, precisely the quantity that predicts generalization. The two gates pull opposite ways and nobody has published how to run both. Counter-camp: synthetic data can *improve* a diffusion model as **negative guidance** rather than training material `[SIMS — arXiv 2408.16333]`.

**Edit-model-driven variation is consensus with zero validation.** Every source asserting an edit model's superiority describes dataset *generation* and stops before training — no LoRA results, no comparison, no artefact discussion `[weirdwonderfulai.art, 2025-10-10; floyo; apatero]`. The mechanistic caveat: preserving identity by construction also preserves the anchor's errors and suppresses variance — the same entropy collapse. **Video-turnaround frame harvesting is dry** — no dated source found, for or against. **Operational cross-link:** a synthetic pipeline on Qwen-Image-Edit in musubi cannot also use caption dropout (PR #1084 rejects it on `--is_edit`), and condition drop is the one mitigation the collapse literature recommends for self-consuming loops.

## 7. Evaluation

**Pairwise-vs-rubric is not a debate the image community has had** — it exists only in NLP/LLM-eval literature and is not cited back. For pairwise: perception "is inherently relative," and it "does not require the user to have global consistency" `[arXiv 2005.10716]`; pairwise evaluators align better with humans than direct scoring `[arXiv 2403.16950]`. Against: the protocol itself induces bias, and pairwise "scale[s] poorly and may produce intransitive judgments" `[arXiv 2504.14716, 2025-04]`. Community practice is labelled XY grids. **No image-community source advocating shuffled/unlabelled comparison for epoch picking was found** — the blind discipline is defensible, not documented consensus.

**Seed-stability and diversity collapse are the consensus overfit detector.** The best-articulated version names four signatures — **prompt inertia**, **skin plasticity**, **pose echoing** ("repeated shoulders/neck angles across varied seeds"), **colour lock** — with a protocol: checkpoint every 200–400 steps; three probes (harsh overhead lighting for texture recovery; a forced busy background for composition flexibility; negative-prompt pressure on "over-smooth skin, plastic texture"); **two consecutive failures ⇒ roll back** `[single-report, vendor blog with first-hand experiments — WaveSpeedAI, 2026-01-23]`.

**Face-embedding tooling has moved; the incumbent is dormant.** `cubiq/ComfyUI_FaceAnalysis` is **"maintenance only" since 2025-04-14**; its calibration recipe is 3 real reference photos scored against a 4th real photo, no thresholds, `filter_thresh` default 100.0 (off) `[official]`. The only successor located is `Kidev/ComfyUI-FaceFilter` — InsightFace `antelopev2`, L2-normalised embeddings, cosine against a reference *set*, **default threshold 0.30**, a filter not a benchmark `[official]` https://github.com/Kidev/ComfyUI-FaceFilter. **No first-hand report of anyone ranking LoRA epochs by FaceEmbedDistance with published calibration numbers.**

**DreamBench++ has 2026 successors, one directly on identity.** DreamBench++ (ICLR 2025): 83.31% / 98.71% human consistency on concept preservation / prompt following, **+32.59 / +37.23 over DINO and CLIP** `[arXiv 2406.16855]`. **MaSC** (2026-05-21) diagnoses the bug as global pooling — "global cosine for CP averages over the whole image, including background variation that humans correctly ignore when judging identity" — with Krippendorff α on DreamBench++ of **MaSC +0.471, GPT-4V +0.432, DINO-I +0.311, CLIP-I +0.135** against a human ceiling of +0.658 `[arXiv 2605.22469]`. That CLIP-I figure is the citable evidence it is near-useless for identity. **DSH-Bench** (2026-04-19) confirms weak CLIP/DINO–human correlation over 459 subjects — but annotates with **absolute scoring**, so the two leading 2026 benchmarks disagree on protocol and neither validates pairwise here `[arXiv 2603.08090v2]`.

**No reusable eval harness for checkpoint selection exists.** `dreambench_plus` evaluates models on a fixed benchmark, not your checkpoints on your subject; `lora-dataset-studio`'s Test Studio does fixed-seed checkpoint × strength grids with votes, Wilson ranking and face ranking but publishes no methodology. Genuine gap.

**One adversarial finding bearing on probe design.** A playbook for *deliberately* overfitting a Civitai-friendly LoRA prescribes maximally similar images, 20–50 of them, repeats 30–50, one epoch, 1024², 32/32, 1,000–2,000 steps — then generating previews from **JoyCaption/Florence captions of the training images themselves**, because that "perfect match between 'training input' and 'generation input'" yields outputs "almost identical to the training images." The author's own verdict: "essentially a dataset image copier" `[single-report — rogerpie, Civitai, 2025-08-21, updated ~2026-04]` https://civitai.com/articles/18443/lets-rapidly-train-an-overfitting-lora. Read as evaluation guidance: any probe overlapping the caption corpus measures recall, and impressive Civitai preview grids are frequently exactly this.

## 8. Video, by analogy

**Wan 2.2: stills work for likeness, clips are mandatory for i2v.** ai-toolkit's Wan 2.2 14B example sets `num_frames: 1` for images `[official]`; diffusion-pipe says for **i2v** you "MUST train on a dataset of only videos," because the first frame is the image conditioning `[official — tdrussell/diffusion-pipe]`. These reconcile — stills for T2V/likeness, not for i2v conditioning.

**Which expert carries likeness is contested.** Official docs give the mechanics — boundary 0.875 T2V / 0.9 I2V — and **no guidance on which expert holds identity** `[official — docs/wan.md]`. Camp A trains both (ai-toolkit alternates via `switch_boundary_every: 10`; published character LoRAs ship separate `_HIGH`/`_LOW` files at 0.7–1.0). Camp B says low-noise carries likeness; diffusion-pipe notes "the low noise model functions adequately when trained across the full timestep range." A first-hand **low-noise-only** identity run: 12–20 images, 1024 long side, 1,200–2,000 steps at LR 5e-5, rank 16–32, "pose diversity over location diversity" `[WaveSpeedAI, 2026-01-23]`. **Image LoRAs do not transfer** — the sanctioned pattern is two LoRAs of the same character, an image one to lock the still and a Wan T2V one for motion.

**MiniMax H3 carries the sharpest official-vs-practice split found.** fal (official, cloud) requires **video clips only**, "never images combined with video in a single dataset" — 10 clips minimum, 50–200 best, 3–15 s at exactly 24.000 fps, buckets in multiples of 32, **frame counts satisfying `frames % 17 == 5`**, ~1,500 steps for ~50 clips, rank 16 — and says its best results were **style/aesthetic adaptation, not identity preservation** `[official — fal, 2026-08-10]` https://fal.ai/learn/devs/how-to-train-a-lora-for-minimax-h3. Two independent local 12 GB runs contradict this in practice: stills-only worked for identity if the set was face-weighted, with `num_frames: 1` **and `auto_frame_count: false`**, or the trainer treats stills as videos and errors "no images found" `[かみもと, 2026-08-07 and 2026-08-17]`. Both can be true if fal's pipeline simply rejects image datasets; practice should follow the local reports, and the **full-body-then-face-only two-phase schedule** is the finding worth carrying.

## Consensus vs contested

**Consensus (multiple independent sources, no dissent found):** 15–40 images for a character LoRA, quality and coverage over volume, near-duplicates and multi-crops of one source actively harmful. Constants-and-uncaptioned become identity; variables must be captioned. Train undistilled, infer distilled — now official at Krea, BFL and kohya. Loss is not the checkpoint selector on flow-matching DiTs; the best rung is rarely the last. Seed-stability collapse and trigger-free bleed are the two reliable overfit tells. Do not upscale sources — trainers downscale and never upscale, so upscaling adds a texture prior and no information. 1024 native long edge is the working target and nothing above it has a published win. DINO/CLIP-I do not track human identity judgement.

**Contested:** the dataset-size knee (30 / 50 / 100 / several hundred, no controlled test). The identity ratio (0.33 vs 0.50, with a successful run at ~0.06 close-ups — probably resolved by face-pixel count). 768 as a training rung on Krea 2 (two Krea-specific community sources bucket 512+768+1024; this repo says 768 was never a trained stage). Rare-token triggers on LLM encoders (BFL officially prescribes them; one Krea-2 source says they surface as watermarks; no A/B). Caption length evenness. Caption dropout (0.05 / 0.1 / 0.3). Rank (16 / 32 / 128-64-64-32, the last style-only). Safe synthetic fraction (5% initiates collapse in a recursive loop vs 60–90% acceptable with entropy gating). Wan 2.2's likeness expert (both vs low-noise only). MiniMax H3 dataset type (clips-only officially vs stills-work locally).
