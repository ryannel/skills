# Adversarial verification — LTX-2.x licence

**Verifier:** independent adversarial pass, 2026-08-22.
**Method:** primary text pulled directly (`curl`) from `raw.githubusercontent.com` and `huggingface.co/.../raw/main/`; AUP PDF downloaded and extracted with `pdftotext`; gating read from the HF API (`gated` field) and the rendered model page. No claim below rests on either prior agent's report. `WebFetch` refused to reproduce the licence verbatim, so all quotes come from the raw files.

Working copies: `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ryannel-Workspace-skill-factory/797c278c-ba5a-439c-886f-b5c762cfa3ec/scratchpad/` (`LICENSE_main.md`, `hf23_LICENSE.txt`, `aup.txt`).

## Verdict

**PARTIALLY REFUTED.** The commercial spine of the original report is correct and I reproduced every load-bearing sentence myself: the LTX-2.x CLA dated August 11 2026 is public and ungated on GitHub, grants a worldwide royalty-free licence, requires a paid licence only from Entities at **$10,000,000 or more** annual revenue aggregated across subsidiaries and affiliates, names **LoRA adapters** as Derivatives, carries the "regardless of who created such Derivative" clause at §3.5, incorporates the AUP by reference in Attachment A, and imposes no territorial restriction beyond OFAC/EAR. The predicted over-read did **not** materialise: the AUP's "Do Not Generate Sexually Explicit Content" sits inside **Universal Usage Standards**, not the API-specific section, and the AUP's own scope covers "on-premises deployments" — so the NSFW prohibition genuinely binds local weights. But the report is wrong or incomplete on four counts that matter for a published skill: it declares the **2.3 licence question settled when the live sources actively conflict**; it says 2.3 is "not gated" when most of the 2.3 family *is*; it claims "no branding requirement" while omitting three separate affirmative disclosure duties; and it silently drops **Attachment A ¶20**, a no-compete clause that is arguably the single most commercially dangerous term in the document. The second agent was right that a contact-info gate exists on 2.5 and did not exist on 2.3's base repo — that is not a contradiction of the first report, and both are partly right.

## What the original report got wrong or overstated

1. **It called the 2.3 licence question settled. It is not — it is actively contradictory, and the report picked one side without flagging the conflict.** The Jan-5 text is physically present in the 2.3 weights repo, but *both* live hyperlinks from that same repo now resolve to the Aug-11 text, and Lightricks replaced the repo-root `LICENSE` on 2026-08-12 with the new text so the old document no longer exists at any HEAD path on GitHub. Meanwhile the new licence's own scope clause, §1.9, says it "is applicable to all LTX-2.5 versions released since August 11, 2026, and all future releases of LTX-2.x under this license" — wording that does *not* reach backwards to 2.3. So the file says one thing, the links say another, and the scope clause says neither. A skill that states flatly "2.3 is governed by the older text" is asserting a resolution the sources do not support. This one matters practically: the old text has **double liquidated damages** and **no non-commercial carve-out**, so which document governs changes a $10M+ studio's exposure materially.

2. **"LTX-2.3 … is not gated" is simply wrong as a family statement.** Fourteen of the eighteen 2.3-family repos carry `gated: auto`, including every IC-LoRA the workbench brief names as the reason 2.3 is still relevant — `Clean-Plate` is gated. The report generalised from the one base repo it checked. This also means the second agent's framing ("a new gate for 2.5 that 2.3 did not have") is itself only half right: the gate was already rolling across 2.3 LoRA repos from 2026-07-26, a fortnight before 2.5 shipped.

3. **"Outputs belong to the user with no branding requirement" is true about branding and misleading about everything adjacent.** There is no "Powered by LTX" obligation — I grepped for it and it is absent. But the report omits that Attachment A ¶5 forbids disseminating output "without expressly and intelligibly disclaiming that the information and/or content is machine generated"; that §6 forbids removing "watermarking, content provenance, latent disclosure" applied to Output and lets Lightricks **revoke the licence immediately** if it merely "reasonably believes" you did; and that §5's grant is bounded by "Except as set forth herein" and "No use of the Output can contravene any provision as stated in the Agreement." "Outputs are yours, free and clear" is the impression the report leaves, and it is not what §5 delivers.

4. **It read Attachment A as nothing but an AUP pointer and missed the twenty enumerated restrictions that follow it** — in particular **¶20**, which is the commercially sharpest clause in the whole licence: you may not use LTX-2.x "in any product, service, or application that directly competes with Licensor's commercial products or services, or is designed to replace or substitute Licensor's offerings in the market, without obtaining a separate commercial license." Lightricks ships consumer video-editing and generative-video apps, so ¶20 plausibly bites on a large share of what someone would build with an open video model — regardless of revenue, and with no $10M floor. Also dropped: **¶17** (no military, weapons, or nuclear applications), **¶18** (no training other models, "For commercial use only"), and **¶19** (no circumventing watermarking or content filters). A licence summary that lists the $10M bar and the NSFW ban but not ¶20 understates the constraint badly.

5. **A conflict inside the documents that neither agent noticed.** Attachment A ¶18 limits the no-training-other-models restriction to commercial use ("For commercial use only: To train, improve, or fine-tune any other machine learning model…"). The incorporated AUP, under `Do Not Abuse our Products`, imposes what reads as an unconditional version on everyone: "Use or access our Products or any outputs to develop, modify, fine tune or improve any products or services that compete with our Products, including to develop, fine tune or train any artificial intelligence or machine learning algorithms or models of any kind." Since Attachment A incorporates the AUP "in its entirety", the narrower carve-out and the broader ban sit in the same instrument. A hobbyist distilling LTX output into another model is squarely in that gap.

6. **Minor:** the report says "above that a paid licence is required" without noting §2.2, which lets a Commercial Entity use the model unpaid "solely for a Non-Commercial Purpose" — evaluation, testing, and non-production R&D. The report does mention the carve-out when contrasting with 2.3, but omits it from its own summary of the $10M rule, where it is the more useful half.

## What I could not reach

- **The Commercial Use Agreement itself.** §2.1 defers all pricing to a document "as will be provided by the Licensor", obtainable only by emailing `ltxv-licensing@lightricks.com`. No published fee schedule; §2.1's breach remedy explicitly contemplates "absent published standard fees". So the cost of the paid tier is unknown and unknowable from public sources.
- **Which licence Lightricks actually intends to govern 2.3.** The conflict in item 1 above is not resolvable from the artefacts; it needs a statement from Lightricks. The second agent's observation that staff answered technical questions in a public thread while ignoring licence questions is consistent with this being unresolved internally, but that is inference about behaviour, not evidence about the text, and I give it no weight beyond "do not expect a quick clarification."
- **Whether the `gated: auto` approval is genuinely automatic in practice.** The API field says `auto` and HF documents that as instant approval on acceptance, but I did not authenticate, so I did not test the flow end to end. I also could not see what Lightricks does with the harvested contact data beyond the marketing-consent language quoted above.
- **Any licence text behind the gate.** If the gated 2.5 repo ships an in-repo `LICENSE` differing from GitHub's — as the ungated 2.3 repo demonstrably does — I could not see it. Given that exact discrepancy exists on 2.3, this is a live risk, not a theoretical one.

## Corrected statement

> LTX-2.5 ships under the **LTX-2.x Community License Agreement** (11 Aug 2026), readable ungated at `github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2/blob/main/LICENSE.md`; the weights need a Hugging Face contact-info gate that also asks for marketing consent. Use is worldwide and royalty-free **below $10M annual revenue**, aggregated across affiliates (§1.6); at or above it a paid licence is required, except for evaluation and non-production R&D (§2.2). Derivatives — LoRAs explicitly (§3.5) — inherit the licence, and the $10M duty follows a LoRA to any commercial recipient "regardless of who created such Derivative." Outputs are yours (§5), but you must disclose content as machine-generated (Att. A ¶5) and must not strip watermarks (§6). **Attachment A ¶20 bars competing with Lightricks' own products at any revenue**; ¶17 bars military use. The incorporated AUP bans sexually explicit generation — universally, including local weights. **LTX-2.3's licence is unsettled**: its repo ships the older Jan-2026 text (double liquidated damages, no non-commercial carve-out) while its own links point at the new one.
