# Wan 2.2

Wan 2.2 is the Alibaba Tongyi Wan team's open-weights video generation family. It was released in **August 2025** under **Apache 2.0 for both code and weights** — genuinely permissive, commercial use included. The headline architecture is a **mixture-of-experts diffusion transformer**. The A14B models carry **27B total parameters, 14B active per step**, split into a **high-noise expert** for early denoising (layout, motion, structure) and a **low-noise expert** for late denoising (texture, detail, temporal coherence). The text encoder is **umT5-XXL**. A separate **5B TI2V** model is dense — no expert split — and uses a different, higher-compression VAE.

**The defining trait:** for the 14B models you are not running one model. You are running two in sequence. That single fact shapes the file list, the node graph, the settings, the LoRA count, and the most common way people get subtly wrong output.

**Wan 2.2 is the last open-weights Wan, and may stay that way.** Wan 2.5, 2.6 and 2.7 exist but are **API-only — the weights were pre-announced as open and never published** `[flagged — re-verify]`. Wan 3.0 has been pre-announced as an Apache-2.0 open-weight release. Treat that as unconfirmed until files appear. If you want a Wan you can download, it is 2.2. Note that this is a claim about the Wan family, not about the open video landscape, which has moved on around it. See [Licence & limitations](#licence--limitations) for where Wan 2.2 now sits against [`minimax-h3`](../minimax-h3/) and [`ltx-2-5`](../ltx-2-5/).

## Task-mode selector

Pick the **task** first — that is what you actually know when you arrive. Size is a VRAM constraint you resolve second. That is why it is a column here, not the top-level axis.

| Task mode | 14B (A14B MoE) | 5B (TI2V, dense) | Use when… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I2V** — still → clip | `wan2.2_i2v_{high,low}_noise_14B` | ✅ same file as T2V | **The production workhorse.** You have (or can make) a good still and want it to move |
| **T2V** — text → clip | `wan2.2_t2v_{high,low}_noise_14B` | ✅ same file | No reference image. Weaker in practice than I2V — you give up all spatial control |
| **FLF2V** — first + last frame → clip | `wan2.2_i2v_*` + `WanFirstLastFrameToVideo` | ❌ | You need to hit a specific end state. The reliable way to control a transition |
| **S2V** — audio + still → talking/singing clip | `wan2.2_s2v_14B` (dense 14B, **not** MoE) | ❌ | Lip-sync to an audio track. Length follows the audio |
| **Animate** — drive a character from a reference video | `Wan2_2-Animate-14B` | ❌ | Character animation and replacement — motion transfer from a driving video |
| **Fun Control / Fun Camera / Fun InP** | `wan2.2_fun_*_{high,low}_noise_14B` | ✅ control + inpaint | Pose/depth conditioning, explicit camera moves, first-last-frame inpainting |

**Choosing size:** the 14B is the quality path and needs the two-expert wiring. The **5B is a genuinely different model, not a smaller one** — dense, its own VAE, 24 fps, 121 frames, `uni_pc` sampler. On ~8 GB VRAM, community consensus is that **5B fp16 beats 14B at Q3_K** `[community — wan27.org guide; re-verify]`. Don't quantise the 14B into the ground when a purpose-built small model already exists.

> **S2V, Animate, Fun and VACE are separate downloads**, not modes of the base checkpoint. Fun and VACE come from **Alibaba PAI** — the same team behind the Fun Union ControlNet used by [`z-image`](../z-image/) — also Apache 2.0.

## The one rule that changes everything

**For the 14B, it is two models, not one — and everything downstream doubles.**

This is a wiring rule, not a prompting rule. It earns the slot because getting it wrong doesn't error. Instead it produces output that half-works and looks subtly off — the most expensive kind of mistake to debug.

The denoising schedule is split at **`t_moe`, the timestep corresponding to half of SNR_min** `[official — Wan2.2 technical report]`. Before that boundary the high-noise expert runs. After it, the low-noise expert runs. In ComfyUI that means:

- **Two `Load Diffusion Model` nodes** — one per expert. Never one.
- **Two `KSamplerAdvanced` nodes in series.** The first runs `start_at_step 0 → end_at_step <boundary>` with `add_noise: enable` and **`return_with_leftover_noise: enable`** — it is handing an unfinished latent to the second. The second runs `<boundary> → 10000`, `add_noise: disable`, `return_with_leftover_noise: disable`. Get `return_with_leftover_noise` wrong on the first sampler and the second one starts from a finished image.
- **Two LoRAs per concept.** A Wan 2.2 LoRA is trained and loaded as a high-noise/low-noise pair (see [The two-LoRA rule](#the-two-lora-rule)).
- **Two of every speed LoRA** — `..._high_noise.safetensors` and `..._low_noise.safetensors`, each into its own expert's chain.
- **Steps and CFG are per-expert questions**, not single numbers.

The mnemonic that keeps it straight: **high-noise decides what happens, low-noise decides how it looks.** Motion, layout and structure are settled before the boundary. Nothing after it will rescue a bad composition, and nothing before it will fix mushy texture.

The 5B is dense — one loader, one sampler, one LoRA. If you want the simple graph, that's the reason to pick it.

## Setup & ecosystem

Wan 2.2 runs in **ComfyUI core**. The DiT is **not a checkpoint** — load it with separate nodes: **Load Diffusion Model** (×2 for 14B) + **CLIPLoader** + **Load VAE**.

### File layout

Download the ComfyUI-repackaged files from the `Comfy-Org` Hugging Face repos. Deploying on rented GPUs? [`comfyui-on-runpod`](../comfyui-on-runpod/) owns the volume layout and `extra_model_paths.yaml`. Note that the 14B needs **both expert files present**, so a partially-populated volume fails in a way that looks like a wiring error.

| File | ComfyUI folder | Loader node |
|---|---|---|
| `wan2.2_{t2v,i2v}_high_noise_14B_fp8_scaled.safetensors` | `models/diffusion_models/` | Load Diffusion Model |
| `wan2.2_{t2v,i2v}_low_noise_14B_fp8_scaled.safetensors` | `models/diffusion_models/` | Load Diffusion Model |
| `wan2.2_ti2v_5B_fp16.safetensors` (the 5B) | `models/diffusion_models/` | Load Diffusion Model |
| `wan2.2_s2v_14B_fp8_scaled.safetensors` (speech-to-video) | `models/diffusion_models/` | Load Diffusion Model |
| `umt5_xxl_fp8_e4m3fn_scaled.safetensors` (text encoder, **shared**) | `models/text_encoders/` | CLIPLoader — **type `wan`** |
| **`wan_2.1_vae.safetensors`** — for **all 14B** variants | `models/vae/` | Load VAE |
| **`wan2.2_vae.safetensors`** — for the **5B only** | `models/vae/` | Load VAE |

> **Two traps that are not guessable from the filenames, both verified in the official templates:**
> 1. **The 14B uses the *Wan 2.1* VAE.** Only the 5B uses `wan2.2_vae.safetensors`. It's a VAE-family split inside one release. Feed the 5B's VAE to a 14B graph and you get colour and detail corruption, not an error.
> 2. **The T2V latent node is `EmptyHunyuanLatentVideo`** — borrowed from an entirely different model family. I2V uses `WanImageToVideo`, FLF2V uses `WanFirstLastFrameToVideo`, and the 5B uses `Wan22ImageToVideoLatent`.

**Universal node settings:** `ModelSamplingSD3` sits on each model path before its sampler. Shift is **8** on the full-step path and **5** on the 4-step speed-LoRA path. The official templates change it along with the LoRA, and it is easy to miss.

### Quantisation & VRAM

- **Official:** fp8-scaled builds of every 14B variant (the filenames above); the 5B ships fp16.
- **Community:** GGUF Q3–Q8 from City96 / QuantStack, requiring the `ComfyUI-GGUF` custom node. **Q4_K_M is the usual sweet spot for the 14B on 12 GB** `[community — re-verify]`.
- Below ~12 GB, prefer the 5B over a deeply-quantised 14B (see the selector note above).

Full quant tables, block-swap/offload guidance and measured VRAM figures: **`references/setup-and-workflows.md`**.

### diffusers

`WanPipeline` (T2V) and `WanImageToVideoPipeline` (I2V) use model IDs `Wan-AI/Wan2.2-T2V-A14B-Diffusers`, `Wan-AI/Wan2.2-I2V-A14B-Diffusers`, and the TI2V-5B equivalent. T2V, I2V and TI2V are all integrated. `[official-via-docs]`

## Per-mode settings

Every number below is **verbatim from the official ComfyUI templates** unless marked otherwise. Where a template ships both a quality path and a speed path, both are given. They are not interchangeable, and they use *different shift values*.

### 14B — I2V, T2V, FLF2V

| | Quality path | Speed path (lightx2v 4-step LoRA) |
|---|---|---|
| Steps (total) | **20** | **4** |
| Expert split | high `0 → 10`, low `10 → 10000` | high `0 → 2`, low `2 → end` |
| CFG | **3.5–4.0** | **1.0** |
| Sampler / scheduler | `euler` / `simple` | `euler` / `simple` |
| `ModelSamplingSD3` shift | **8** | **5** |
| Frames / fps | **81 @ 16 fps** (≈5 s) | same |
| Default resolution | 640×640 in-template; **480p and 720p** are the supported bands | same |

On the first (high-noise) sampler: `add_noise: enable`, `return_with_leftover_noise: enable`. On the second: `add_noise: disable`, `return_with_leftover_noise: disable`.

### 5B TI2V (dense)

- **20 steps, CFG 5.0, `uni_pc` / `simple`, shift 8** — note the *different sampler* from the 14B.
- **121 frames @ 24 fps**, `Wan22ImageToVideoLatent`, **1280×704 / 704×1280**, 720p only.

### S2V-14B (dense, audio-driven)

- **10 steps, CFG 6.0, `uni_pc` / `simple`, shift 8**, `WanSoundImageToVideo`, **77 frames @ 16 fps**.
- Single `Load Diffusion Model` — S2V is a dense 14B, **not** an A14B MoE. Clip length follows the audio.

### The speed LoRAs, and their tax

`wan2.2_{i2v,t2v}_lightx2v_4steps_lora_v1*_{high,low}_noise.safetensors` goes in `models/loras/`, one per expert, strength 1.0 in the stock templates. It gives roughly a **20× speed-up** at 4 steps with no CFG `[official — lightx2v]`. The official ComfyUI templates now ship with this path enabled by default.

**The tax is motion.** Both lightx2v and ComfyUI acknowledge that the 4-step LoRA reduces dynamism. The characteristic symptom is **slow-motion output** `[official — lightx2v discussion #26; ComfyUI blog]`. Named community fixes, in rough order of effectiveness:

1. **Split the strengths and re-introduce CFG on the high-noise expert only** — LoRA ~0.6–0.8 on high / 1.0 on low, CFG ~2–3.5 on high / 1.0 on low, and **8 steps (4 high + 4 low)** instead of 4 `[community — re-verify]`. Motion lives in the high-noise expert, so that is where the guidance needs to come back.
2. **Raise the resolution.** 1280×720 produces normal-speed motion where 832×480 produces slow motion. That is counter-intuitive, since 480p is the "recommended" band `[community — re-verify]`.
3. **Fall back to the 20-step quality path** when the shot is motion-critical. Reach for the speed path to find composition and seed, then re-render the keepers.

### Community merges, and why their settings contradict the templates

The Apache-2.0 licence has produced a large secondary market of Wan 2.2 merges — speed-distilled, motion-tuned, concept-merged. **Their settings are not the template's settings.** Every way of getting that wrong fails silently. Three differences recur, all of them visible in the published workflow of one studied 10-step I2V merge `[community — RedMimicStudios]`:

- **Asymmetric CFG — 2.0 on the high-noise expert, 1.0 on the low.** This is not a typo. It follows from the merge being **CFG-distilled**: guidance is already baked into the weights, so asking for it again applies it twice, which burns contrast and crisps texture. The residual 2.0 on high is the same repair as the strength split above. Distillation flattens dynamism, and motion is decided before the expert boundary, so guidance comes back only on the side that decides motion.
- **`uni_pc` / `normal`, not `euler` / `simple`** — and the author reports this mattered. At 10 total steps (5 per expert) the solver's per-step truncation error is a first-order term in the picture, not a rounding detail. `uni_pc` is a higher-order multistep solver that stays accurate on a schedule that coarse, where `euler` does not. Wan's own low-step templates already agree: the 10-step S2V path and the 5B both ship `uni_pc`.
- **No LoRA loaded at all.** A merge with speed or concept LoRAs baked in already carries their deltas. Loading the same LoRA over it applies the delta a second time — `W + 2αBA` where the merge intended `W + αBA`. That puts the weights off the distribution the merge was tuned on: an over-baked concept, and for a speed LoRA a doubled dose of the dynamism loss you were trying to avoid.

**Read the workflow JSON, not the post.** Both the asymmetric CFG split and the `uni_pc` choice were present in that merge's published workflow and absent from its description text `[community — RedMimicStudios]`. The full settings block, VRAM and timings, and the measurements that picked the merge, are in **`references/setup-and-workflows.md §4a`**.

## Prompting Wan 2.2

The full anatomy, camera vocabulary and worked examples are in **`references/prompting-guide.md`**. The three things that matter most:

**Describe the action, not the scene.** umT5 will happily encode a still-life description, and you will get a still-life that barely moves. A prompt carried over from an image model is the single most common cause of "my video doesn't move." Name the motion, its direction, and its speed.

**Camera language is literal.** `dolly in`, `pan left`, `orbit`, `crane up`, `handheld` all work as written. To *stop* camera motion, say so explicitly — `static shot`, `fixed camera` — because the model's default is to add drift.

**Negatives exist here, and the default is Chinese.** Unlike the guidance-distilled image models in this suite, the 14B quality path runs real CFG (3.5–4.0), so negative prompts do work. Wan ships a long default Chinese negative prompt. Community consensus is to **keep it in Chinese rather than translate it**, because the encoder was trained on it in that form `[community — re-verify]`. On the 4-step speed path at CFG 1.0, negatives go inert, exactly as they do on any guidance-off model.

**Prompt extension** is a first-class official feature: `--use_prompt_extend --prompt_extend_method 'dashscope'|'local_qwen'`, backed by Qwen2.5-Instruct models (VL variants for I2V) `[official]`. It measurably enriches detail. Locally, any competent LLM does the same job.

## The default is motion, not stillness — and the look is graded

Two defaults define what Wan 2.2 hands you when you do not ask for anything, and both run opposite to the habits an image model teaches.

**The motion default: it drifts.** Left unspecified, the camera moves and the subject fidgets. Every still-image workflow in this suite assumes the frame holds unless you request motion. Wan assumes the opposite, so an unstated camera is not a locked-off one. `static shot` / `fixed camera` is a thing you *type*, not a thing you get by omission. That one phrase fixes the floating product shot and half of the "why won't my locked frame stay locked" reports. The lever in the other direction is the high-noise expert: motion is decided before the expert boundary. That is why the 4-step speed LoRA (a distillation of that expert) flattens dynamism, and why the fix for flat motion is CFG and steps on the *high* side rather than a more energetic prompt. See [The speed LoRAs, and their tax](#the-speed-loras-and-their-tax).

**The aesthetic default: it grades.** Wan 2.2 was trained with curated aesthetic labels for lighting, composition, contrast and colour tone `[official — Wan2.2 technical report]`. The visible result is a mild cinematic grade — lifted contrast, warm-cool separation, shallow depth — applied whether or not you asked. For narrative shots that is free production value. For anything that must match existing footage, a phone-video look, or a flat-graded plate, name the target explicitly (`flat log profile`, `unlit fluorescent office`, `consumer phone footage`) and expect to fight it. The prior sits in the weights, not in the prompt.

Full motion and camera vocabulary, including the qualifiers that read as intended: **`references/prompting-guide.md §3–4`**.

## Motion, camera and structural control

Wan 2.2's control stack — Fun Camera, Fun Control, Fun InP, VACE — is the deepest in the open video ecosystem. It is the durable reason to reach for Wan even where [`minimax-h3`](../minimax-h3/) or [`ltx-2-5`](../ltx-2-5/) win on raw quality or native audio. The comparison is about depth, not existence, and it is worth stating precisely, because this is the advantage that survives the next quality release. **MiniMax H3 ships no conditioning rig at all** — its control surface is the prompt. **LTX-2.5 does ship one**: its IC-LoRAs take a reference input alongside the text and apply frame-level spatial control with an optional mask, covering depth, canny, pose and motion tracks. What Wan still holds over it is maturity and breadth. Most of LTX's adapters are 2.3-trained with only one 2.5-native, its camera-move LoRAs are LTX-2-era files that may be stale `[flagged — re-verify]`, and **no other open model in the suite has an explicit camera rig at all**. Reach past Wan for control only when LTX's reference-plus-mask shape is the better fit for the job.

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Fun Camera Control** | Explicit camera moves — pan up/down/left/right, zoom in/out, and combinations. `alibaba-pai/Wan2.2-Fun-A14B-Control-Camera` |
| **Fun Control** | Pose / depth / structural video conditioning — the ControlNet analogue |
| **Fun InP** | First-last-frame controlled generation with smooth interpolation between them |
| **VACE (Fun)** | Reference-driven consistency — the **no-training** path to a consistent character pose |
| **Animate** | Motion transfer and character replacement from a driving video |

All are separate `high_noise` / `low_noise` file pairs for the 14B and wire into the same two-expert graph. Details, file tables and workflow wiring: **`references/motion-and-camera.md`**.

### SCAIL-2 and Bernini-R — Wan-derived, from other labs

"Which Wan-family model replaces a character in existing footage" is a question this skill gets asked, and the answer is no longer Animate. Two models share Wan's lineage without being Wan 2.2 or Alibaba releases.

**SCAIL-2** comes from **zai-org (Z.ai / Zhipu AI)** — a different lab. It is a **full fine-tune of the Wan2.1-14B-I2V weights** on a modified Wan 2.1 DiT that stacks 28 extra in-context conditioning channels and Mode-Specific RoPE `[official — arXiv 2606.10804 §4.1]`. It does reference-image plus driving-video character animation and replacement with SAM3 identity tracking, through the core ComfyUI node `WanSCAILToVideo` `[official-via-docs]`, and has displaced Animate for that job in community practice `[community — 2026-08; convergent]`. Licence splits by artefact: **Apache 2.0** code, **MIT** weights. **Bernini-R** (ByteDance — announced, not covered by this suite) does the same reference-driven job, built on **Wan 2.2**, Apache 2.0.

State the lineage precisely, because it decides something practical. SCAIL-2 is Wan **2.1** with extra input channels, so a Wan 2.2 two-expert LoRA will not load into it. Even a Wan 2.1 LoRA that touches the patch embedding will not transfer `[flagged — re-verify]`. That follows from the architecture, and **nobody has tested it**, so try a throwaway generation before you plan around it. [`scail-2`](../scail-2/) tracks the open question.

Full coverage — architecture, the mask-conditioning contract, the reference-prep craft that decides whether it works at all, and ecosystem tooling: [`scail-2`](../scail-2/).

## The two-LoRA rule

**Train and load two LoRAs — one per expert — from the same dataset.** This is the position the trainer authors and the Civitai training articles converge on `[community — musubi-tuner #455, Civitai guides; convergent]`. It follows directly from the architecture: a LoRA patches a specific set of weights, and the two experts are different weights.

`musubi-tuner` (kohya-ss) is the default trainer and exposes this explicitly via a `dit_high_noise` path alongside `dit`, plus `timestep_boundary` and `discrete_flow_shift`. Reported starting points are **rank 32 / alpha 16, LR ~2e-4, batch 1–2 on 16–24 GB**, or rank 64 for detailed identity. Some authors run a higher LR (~3e-4) on the high-noise LoRA when they only need motion and layout `[community — musubi-tuner #455, Civitai guides; re-verify]`.

**Contested:** whether you can skip the high-noise LoRA. Training only the low-noise expert is cheaper, and some authors report acceptable results for pure appearance work. But it cannot by construction affect motion or layout, which are settled before the boundary. Treat single-LoRA training as an appearance-only shortcut, not a general substitute `[contested]`.

**A known gotcha:** musubi-tuner has an open report of a two-expert run emitting only one LoRA file (issue #569) — check you actually got both artefacts before concluding the training worked `[flagged — re-verify]`.

Full hyperparameters, dataset construction, single-frame vs video-clip training, and evaluation: **`references/lora-training.md`**.

## Production pipelines & mixing models

**The handoff that matters: lock the still first.** Wan 2.2's I2V path is stronger than its T2V path. An image model gives you far more control over composition, identity and style than any video prompt will. The suite pipeline is:

> **lock a still** ([`z-image`](../z-image/), [`flux-2`](../flux-2/), [`sdxl`](../sdxl/), [`krea-2`](../krea-2/)) → **Wan 2.2 I2V** → **restore/upscale** → **interpolate** → deliver

**Restore and upscale *before* you interpolate**, with a temporally-aware restorer (SeedVR2, FlashVSR) rather than a per-frame image upscaler. [`image-production-workflows`](../image-production-workflows/) owns that ordering rule and the reasoning behind it, along with the rest of the cross-model handoff craft — denoise bands, decode-to-pixels between VAE families, tiled upscale.

What is specific to Wan is how much weight the last rung carries. The 14B is built around **81 frames at 16 fps**, which is below the rate anything else in the chain expects. Interpolation is therefore a standard stage here, not optional polish, and RIFE to 30 or 60 is the normal finish. Per-stage settings and segment stitching for longer pieces are in **`references/setup-and-workflows.md`**.

## Failure modes & QC

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Clip barely moves; subject drifts in slow motion | The 4-step speed LoRA suppresses dynamism — motion is decided in the high-noise expert, and the distillation flattens it | Split strengths (0.6–0.8 high / 1.0 low), restore CFG 2–3.5 on high only, 8 steps; or raise to 720p; or drop to the 20-step path |
| Output looks fine but subtly soft / wrong texture | High and low expert files mismatched, or a LoRA loaded into only one expert | Verify both loaders point at the *same* variant and both LoRA halves are loaded |
| Second sampler produces a fully re-noised mess | `return_with_leftover_noise` wrong on the high-noise sampler, or `add_noise` left enabled on the low-noise one | First sampler: leftover noise **enable**; second: `add_noise` **disable** |
| Colour shift or mush across the whole clip | 5B VAE used on a 14B graph (or vice versa) | 14B → `wan_2.1_vae.safetensors`; 5B → `wan2.2_vae.safetensors` |
| Anatomy warps or collapses partway through the clip; a handful of seeds are clean and most are not, and no setting changes the ratio | The checkpoint's prior does not cover that composition, so which seed lands inside it decides the outcome. Steps, shift, frame count, negatives and LoRA strength all steer the trajectory through the *same* prior and cannot widen it | Stop sweeping: swap to a checkpoint or merge trained on that subject matter, then re-run one generation. See [When nothing you change moves the result](#when-nothing-you-change-moves-the-result) |
| Late frames go smooth and plastic — anatomy holds but linework and texture are gone; upscaling doesn't bring them back | A merge tuned for motion smoothness suppresses high-frequency detail at every step. I2V compounds that frame-on-frame, so the last frames have nothing left to restore — a restorer can only sharpen detail that survived | Measure edge density first frame vs last before reaching for a restorer; if the decay is deep, change the merge rather than the post chain |
| Identity morphs partway through the clip | Conditioning influence decays as the clip progresses; no persistent identity anchor past the opening frames | Shorter clips; VACE/Animate reference conditioning; character LoRA |
| Colour pumping and drift across extended segments | Error accumulation — each extension conditions on an already-degraded final frame, compounded by per-segment VAE round-trips | Re-anchor on a clean keyframe; FLF2V to a known end state; colour-match segments |
| Visible seam at a segment join | Independently generated segments with a motion discontinuity at the boundary | Overlap frames, or FLF-condition each segment on its neighbour |
| Negative prompt ignored | You are on the 4-step path at CFG 1.0 — guidance-off, so negatives are inert | Phrase constraints positively, or move to the CFG 3.5–4.0 quality path |
| Shimmer on fine detail after upscaling | A per-frame image upscaler was used on video — no cross-frame consistency | Use a temporal restorer (SeedVR2 / FlashVSR); upscale before interpolating |

### When nothing you change moves the result

A 27-generation single-variable study into anatomy collapsing mid-clip on I2V is worth carrying in full, because its conclusion generalises well past the symptom it started from `[community — RedMimicStudios]`. With the seed held fixed, none of the following changed the outcome: prompt phrasing and eight anti-distortion negatives; 20 vs 12 steps; an NSFW LoRA at strength 1.0 vs bypassed; 49 vs 33 frames; shift at 6.5, 7.0, 8.0 and 9.0; and three retouched revisions of the source image, each under 1/255 mean pixel delta from the last. The only axis that moved anything at all was the seed. A 28-run seed batch returned **2 usable clips out of 28**.

> **The diagnostic: when seed variance dominates and nothing deterministic moves the needle, the base model cannot hold that composition — change the checkpoint rather than continuing to roll.** Every deterministic knob steers the trajectory through the same learned prior. None of them widens it. Once the prior does not cover what you are asking for, sweeping settings is just resampling with extra steps. In the study, swapping to an NSFW-merged checkpoint fixed it in a single generation, after 27 generations of tuning had not.

**And measure detail decay rather than eyeballing it.** Edge density on the first frame against the last is a cheap QC proxy for how much high-frequency detail a clip loses as it plays. It discriminates cleanly: the checkpoint that worked held **−3.2%**, while the two that failed ran **−10.1%** and **−27.3%** — in opposite ways, one breaking anatomy and one dissolving linework. It also settles whether post-processing can rescue a clip. ESRGAN on the −27.3% output moved it to −28.2%, i.e. nowhere, proving by measurement that the late frames had no detail left to recover, rather than being merely blurred. The full comparison, and the settings of the merge that fixed it, are in **`references/setup-and-workflows.md §4a`**.

## Pre-flight checklist

1. Task mode chosen first, size second?
2. **14B: both experts loaded**, and both LoRA halves if using any LoRA?
3. `return_with_leftover_noise` **enable** on the high-noise sampler, `add_noise` **disable** on the low-noise one?
4. Correct VAE for the size — `wan_2.1_vae` for 14B, `wan2.2_vae` for 5B?
5. CLIPLoader type set to **`wan`**?
6. Right latent node for the mode — `EmptyHunyuanLatentVideo` (T2V) / `WanImageToVideo` (I2V) / `WanFirstLastFrameToVideo` (FLF2V) / `Wan22ImageToVideoLatent` (5B)?
7. Shift matched to the path — **8** full-step, **5** on the 4-step LoRA?
8. Frame count and fps matched to the model — 81 @ 16 for 14B, 121 @ 24 for 5B?
9. Prompt names **motion**, not just a scene? Camera move stated, or `static shot` if you want none?
10. If motion looks slow: tried strength-split + CFG-on-high before blaming the prompt?
11. Post chain ordered **restore/upscale → interpolate**, not the reverse?
12. On a community merge: settings read out of its **workflow JSON** rather than its description, and **no LoRA stacked** on top of one already merged in?

## Licence & limitations

**Licence:** **Apache 2.0 for code and weights** `[official — repo LICENSE and technical report]`. Commercial use permitted. The repo adds an acceptable-use statement (no unlawful content, no harm, no misinformation, no targeting vulnerable groups) and places accountability on the user. That is guidance alongside the licence, not a restriction in it. The PAI **Fun** and **VACE** derivatives are also Apache 2.0. Verify the licence on any community finetune separately.

**Release timeline:** Wan 2.2 shipped **August 2025**. Wan 2.5 / 2.6 / 2.7 followed as **API-only**; their weights were pre-announced as open and never published `[flagged — re-verify]`. Wan 3.0 is pre-announced as Apache-2.0 open weights — unconfirmed.

**Where Wan 2.2 stands as of August 2026.** It is no longer the *default* open video model in community practice. [`minimax-h3`](../minimax-h3/) took that position on release and dominates the discussion, with [`ltx-2-5`](../ltx-2-5/) the other pole. That is a change in default, not a demotion. The position Wan 2.2 holds instead is a durable one: **the Apache-2.0, controllable, trainable open video model.** All three of its advantages are structural, not a quality lead someone can beat next month: a licence with no territory clause, no revenue threshold and no field-of-use restriction; the deepest conditioning rig in the open field (Fun Camera / Fun Control / Fun InP / VACE), and the only explicit camera rig anywhere in the suite (H3 has no rig at all, and [`ltx-2-5`](../ltx-2-5/)'s IC-LoRAs cover pose and depth but not a camera path); and a mature two-expert LoRA ecosystem with a first-class trainer behind it. H3 is territory-excluded and LTX is revenue-gated and bars explicit content, so for a large share of readers Wan 2.2 is not merely the fallback. It is the only one of the three they may lawfully ship. Real production work now routinely mixes them: a widely-shared 6–8-month project generated most shots on Wan 2.1/2.2 and swapped several to H3 in the final week `[community — LastCrusaderVHS]`. **Reach for Wan 2.2 when the work must be licence-clean, precisely controlled, or trained on your own material. Reach past it when raw fidelity or native audio is the whole job and the licence clears you.**

**Length:** the 14B is built around ~5-second clips (81 frames @ 16 fps). Longer pieces are a stitching problem, not a parameter — expect drift and plan to re-anchor.

**No generated audio.** S2V consumes an audio track for lip-sync; it does not produce one.

## How to read the claims in this skill — two bars, by claim type

This skill holds two kinds of claim to two different standards, because they fail in two different ways.

**Hard facts — must be exact or it breaks.** The Apache-2.0 licence, the MoE architecture and the `t_moe` = half-SNR_min boundary, 27B total / 14B active, exact filenames, the 14B↔`wan_2.1_vae` / 5B↔`wan2.2_vae` split, node names (`EmptyHunyuanLatentVideo`, `WanImageToVideo`, `WanFirstLastFrameToVideo`, `Wan22ImageToVideoLatent`, `WanSoundImageToVideo`, `ModelSamplingSD3`), the CLIPLoader `wan` type, the diffusers pipeline classes, and **every number in the per-mode settings tables** — all come from the official Wan2.2 repo/technical report and are read **verbatim out of the official ComfyUI template JSONs** (`Comfy-Org/workflow_templates`), not from documentation prose, which omits most of them. A wrong filename 404s. A wrong VAE corrupts output silently. These are also the **volatile** ones — template details, quant filenames and speed-LoRA versions move week to week. **Re-verify before relying on them, regardless of who said it.**

**Craft — what actually makes a good clip.** The slow-motion fixes, the strength-split and CFG-on-high recipe, the resolution effect, LoRA hyperparameters, the restore-before-interpolate ordering, dataset construction, segment-stitching practice. **The authoritative source here is the community** — the trainers and workflow authors named in the markers throughout this skill: **wan27.org**'s guide authors on the size and quantisation trade, **LastCrusaderVHS** on mixed-model production practice, the **musubi-tuner** training discussion (#455) and the **Civitai** training articles on the two-expert LoRA question, the **lightx2v** HF discussion thread on the speed-LoRA slow-motion tax, and **RedMimicStudios** on the anatomy-collapse study, the change-the-checkpoint diagnostic and the edge-density QC proxy. These are people who have run this model thousands of times, not the model card, which ships one example and moves on. It is stated with confidence; ranges mean "your resolution and dataset differ from the author's," not "this is unreliable."

Three points held as genuinely contested or unverified:

- **Single-LoRA training** — whether a low-noise-only LoRA is good enough. It cannot affect motion by construction, but authors report acceptable appearance-only results. `[contested]`
- **The slow-motion recipes and the resolution effect** — widely reported and reproducible, but the exact strength/CFG numbers vary by author and the underlying cause is still being worked on upstream. `[community — re-verify]`
- **Wan 2.5+ weights and Wan 3.0** — currently API-only and unreleased respectively. This is the fastest-moving claim in the skill and a landing would substantially change the "which model" advice. `[flagged — re-verify]`

**Facts dated 2026-08-13**; community craft and suite positioning refreshed 2026-08-22. The ComfyUI template numbers, quant filenames and speed-LoRA versions move fastest, and the "which open video model" verdict moves almost as fast. Re-verify all of them before relying on them.
